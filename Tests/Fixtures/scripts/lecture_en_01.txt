Thanks. Let me pick up with the data, since everything else depends on it.
The dataset we use this term is called Meadowlark, and it contains about forty thousand labelled images of birds.
Each image comes with a species label and a rough bounding box drawn by volunteers.
We hold out one in every five images for validation and never touch them during training.
The labels are noisy, so expect some birds to be filed under the wrong species.
That noise is one reason we lean on regularisation later in the lecture.
Now, the model itself is a small convolutional network with five blocks.
Each block has two convolutions, a normalisation layer, and a pooling step.
For the optimizer we use Adam, because it is forgiving when the learning rate is not perfectly tuned.
Plain stochastic gradient descent can work too, but it needs much more patience.
The learning rate warms up over the first two thousand steps and then decays slowly.
Without the warm up, the first few hundred updates tend to be wild.
Let me add something about memory, because people always run out of it.
We train with a batch size of two hundred fifty six on a single graphics card.
If your card is smaller, halve the batch and accumulate gradients over two steps.
The result is nearly identical, it just takes a little longer.
Mixed precision also helps, and it rarely hurts accuracy on a task like this.
Next, regularisation, which is where most of the real work happens.
We apply dropout with a rate of ten percent after the last two blocks.
We also add a little weight decay, which keeps the weights from growing without limit.
And we flip and crop the images at random, so the network never sees exactly the same picture twice.
Together these tricks matter more than any change to the architecture.
Now let us talk about stability, which is the part that usually goes wrong at two in the morning.
To stop exploding gradients we use gradient clipping with a maximum norm of one.
If you see the loss jump to infinity, check that clipping is switched on before anything else.
We also log the gradient norm every hundred steps so problems show up early.
So how long do we train? In our runs the validation loss stopped improving after epoch twelve.
After that point the training loss keeps falling, but the model is only memorising.
That is why we keep the checkpoint with the best validation score, not the last one.
