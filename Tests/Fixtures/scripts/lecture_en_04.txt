Welcome to the software project course, and thank you for coming to the first session.
This term you will build a real application for a real client, from the first interview to the final demo.
The client for this year's project is the city library, and they need a new system for room bookings.
They will join us twice during the term to review what you have built.
Let me go through how the teams work.
You will work in teams of four students, and we form the teams by the end of this week.
Each team chooses a team lead, but the role rotates every three weeks.
That way everyone gets some practice at planning and at saying no.
In the first two weeks you will interview the librarians and write down what they actually need.
Do not start coding before you understand the problem, it is the most common mistake we see.
Write the requirements as short user stories, and check each one with the client.
Now the tools, which are not optional.
All code must live in the university GitLab, not on personal accounts.
We look at the commit history when we grade, so commit early and commit often.
Every change needs approval from two teammates before it is merged.
Reviews are where most of the learning happens, so take them seriously.
On testing, we have a simple rule.
The target for automated test coverage is seventy percent of the code.
Coverage is not quality, but a project with no tests is not finished.
We will show you how to run the tests automatically on every push.
Next, the rhythm of the week.
There is a short stand up meeting every Monday at nine, and it takes fifteen minutes at most.
Each person says what they did, what they will do next, and what is blocking them.
If something is blocking you for more than a day, tell us, do not wait for the meeting.
Keep a shared board with all open tasks, and move each card when its status changes.
We will look at the board during the weekly meetings, so keep it honest.
A board full of cards marked almost done usually means nothing is done at all.
Some practical matters about money.
Each team gets a budget of five hundred euros for hardware, such as tablets for testing.
Keep the receipts, the finance office is very strict about them.
Now the important dates.
Demo day is on December the third, in the main hall, and the client will be in the audience.
Each team gets ten minutes to present and five minutes for questions.
Rehearse the demo at least twice, and always have a backup video in case the network fails.
Show the client the thing they asked for first, and the clever extras afterwards.
Every member of the team should speak during the presentation.
After the demo you hand in a written report.
The final report is limited to twenty pages, including figures.
Shorter is fine, and clearer is always better.
Late submissions lose five percent of the mark for every day they are late.
That is the overview, now let us take your questions.
